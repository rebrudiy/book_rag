# Corpus sources and rights

Book RAG keeps source URLs, edition descriptions, work identities, language,
retrieval dates, rights statements and source hashes. The software's MIT license
**does not apply to book texts**. The bundled demo has its own provenance.

## Bundled reproducible demo

`corpus/demo/manifest.json` describes eight works in English, French, German and
Russian. Seven are explicitly labelled **excerpts**; the Pushkin poem is complete.
These are intended for installation and deterministic retrieval evaluation, not
as a claim that eight full books have been ingested. `provenance.json` records
original source-file SHA-256 hashes, extraction anchors, paragraph counts and
redistributed-file hashes. Downloaded originals live under ignored
`data/source-audit/`. Source excerpts were copied from actual downloaded texts,
not reconstructed from memory or generated by a model.

The demo covers fiction (Carroll, Austen, Kafka), philosophy (Marcus Aurelius,
Voltaire), science (Darwin), economics (Smith) and Russian poetry (Pushkin).
Each linked catalog identifies the specific electronic edition. The Marcus
Aurelius sample uses **Meric Casaubon's translation**, as identified by the notes
inside that electronic text. Darwin's sample uses the 1859 first edition.
Historical print-edition descriptions are transcribed from the text where clear;
the electronic edition remains the exact reproducible citation target.

## Project Gutenberg

Primary policy and catalog evidence reviewed on 2026-11-14:

- [License](https://www.gutenberg.org/policy/license.html)
- [Permissions and jurisdiction](https://www.gutenberg.org/policy/permission.html)
- [Robot access and harvesting](https://www.gutenberg.org/policy/robot_access.html)
- [Offline catalogs and metadata](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/offline_catalogs.html)
- [Machine-readable catalog files](https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/feeds/)

`corpus/manifest.json` contains 20 verified downloadable full electronic editions
of 19 works. Darwin's first and sixth editions share a work ID. Every selected
catalog page stated `Public domain in the USA.` on the review date. A new
translation can have different rights from the original: the English Kafka
translation in Gutenberg #5200 is copyrighted and was **excluded**, whereas the
German original #22367 is included. Audio-only Russian catalog records were
excluded from textual acquisition.

Gutenberg's determination concerns the **United States**. It does not establish
permission in every jurisdiction. Users outside the US must check applicable
law before downloading or redistributing. This is retained on every source
record rather than silently treating a provider-wide label as worldwide rights.

Gutenberg's trademark and license conditions are separate from copyright in the
underlying text. The full source license is included as
`corpus/PROJECT_GUTENBERG_LICENSE.txt`; original formats remain freely available
through each record's source and download links. Excerpts are modified extracts,
and normalization is disclosed below. No book text is relicensed under MIT.

### Automated acquisition

Use the provider's offline machine-readable metadata, harvest endpoint or mirrors
for expansion. Do not crawl search/catalog pages to enumerate books. Official
robot policy provides a harvesting example with a **two-second wait** between
requests. The small curated catalog was manually selected and checked; bulk
acquisition must use the offline catalog workflow and bounded request rates.
Download URLs point to generated cache files, not search pages. Full downloads
and indexes are ignored by Git and should remain outside the public repository.

The official CSV has no rights column. **It is not a public-domain allowlist.**
Use the official RDF catalog, whose per-book rights fields can be filtered,
or supply an enriched CSV containing verified rights. Do not infer rights from
language, author age, or a title matching a known public-domain work.

## Parsing and citation semantics

TXT, HTML/XHTML, EPUB, FB2 and text-bearing PDF are supported. EPUB follows the
package spine; FB2 traverses nested sections and keeps the notes body. HTML
headings and footnote text survive extraction. PDF uses labelled pages; scanned
or encrypted PDFs return an actionable import failure. OCR is an explicit future
step rather than silently indexing an empty scan.

Canonical section text uses Unicode NFC, LF newlines, collapsed whitespace
within paragraphs, and two newlines between paragraphs. Wrapped TXT lines join
with a space. HTML styling and images are omitted; footnotes remain text. Exact
citation offsets refer to canonical section text, **not raw source-byte offsets
or a printed page number inferred from layout**. The original source hash and
edition link let a reviewer identify the source artifact independently.

ZIP members are read without filesystem extraction, with member and total size
limits; paths cannot escape the archive. XML uses defusedxml to reject external
entities. Legacy non-UTF-8 TXT files require explicit conversion so quotations
are not silently corrupted. PDF layout, poetry lineation, tables and inline
formatting remain extraction limitations. Dedicated structure-aware rendering
and OCR quality evaluation are planned.
